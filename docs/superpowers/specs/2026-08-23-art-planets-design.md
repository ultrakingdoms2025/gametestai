# Phase 9 · `art-planets` — planet surfaces art pass

**Branch:** `art-planets` (worktree). **Roadmap:** Phase 9, decision D4 (hybrid).
**Budget gate:** Phase 1's. Draw calls, triangles, materials, renderables, instanced meshes,
world lights and **shader programs** measured before and after, in the same six framings, by
the same script, with the **noise floor established first**.

**Evidence:** `docs/superpowers/specs/img/2026-08-23-art-planets/`. The pair that carries this
pass is `before-ejecta-front.jpg` / `after-ejecta-front.jpg`: the largest ejecta block on
Cinder, from seven metres, which is where a player meets one.
`defect-castle-masonry-on-a-boulder.jpg` is the frame where a change made things *worse*, and
it is in the folder because the worse picture is what diagnosed the biggest defect in this
world.

---

## 1. What this world is, and where the defect was

Ten landable planets generated by one class from a frozen descriptor. Cinder is the
reference: 191,729 triangles in 34 renderables over 17 materials and one world light.

The six `VIEWS.cinder` framings are all landscape vantages. **Every one of them is unchanged
to within a tenth of a luma point by everything this branch did** (§6.3). That is the fifth
consecutive branch to find it, and it is why the defect was found at **seven metres from one
rock** instead.

### 1.1 The noise floor, taken before anything was attributed

`art-citadel`'s rule, and it earned itself twice here. The base tree was measured **three**
times on identical code:

| metric | run-to-run swing on unchanged base code |
|---|---|
| `worldTriangles` | **0** in all six framings, all three runs |
| `materials`, `renderables`, `instancedMeshes`, `worldLights`, `geometries` | **0** |
| `drawCalls` | **0** across all three base runs — and that turned out to be luck, see §6.2 |
| `programs` | **+15 and +37** between two runs; a third run never left 225 at all |
| whole-frame luma / Sobel edge | ±0.03 / ±0.08 |

`MazeWorld` seeds itself from `Math.random()`; the planets do not. There is no `Math.random`
anywhere in `PlanetWorld.js`, `planets/**` or `terrain/**`, so two runs of the same commit
photograph the same world. Confirmed rather than assumed.

## 3. What the photograph showed, at seven metres

| the largest ejecta block on Cinder, from 7 m | before |
|---|---|
| mean luma over its own interior | 29.4 / 255 |
| pixels under 48/255 | 87.6% |

Three or four facets across a 1600 px frame, each an unbroken flat value, with a hard seam
down the middle of the lit one. It reads as a piece of masonry.

**And the triangle count is not the fault.** Twenty triangles is a reasonable budget for a
thing that exists 1,100 times on one planet. The faults are:

1. **It is a regular convex solid.** All 8,880 boulders in the game are the same twenty faces
   meeting five-at-a-vertex in the same pattern, so the full 3-D per-instance tumble
   `buildPropField` already applies **rotates a symmetry instead of changing a silhouette**.
2. **Its UVs are the polyhedron's spherical ones**, which pinch to a point at the poles and
   carry a wrap seam. Texel density varied by an order of magnitude across one rock.

## 4. The defect that only became visible by making things worse

The authored block's first build gave every face its own UV island, unwrapped onto a basis
taken from that face's own first edge. The rock came out looking like **a paved stone
terrace** — hard bright parallel lines in a regular ladder across the whole body
(`defect-castle-masonry-on-a-boulder.jpg`).

Two separate things, and the second is the biggest defect in this world:

- **The islands needed a rotation, not just an offset.** Adjacent facets were sampling in
  nearly the same direction, the texture is anisotropic, and its lines ran on across facet
  boundaries. Fixed with a seeded per-face angle. Measured as the circular mean resultant of
  the per-face UV gradient: **0.93 → 0.31** (1.0 is every face aligned).

- **The bright lines were MORTAR COURSES.** `PlanetWorld._propMaterial` cloned
  **`stone.castle`**, which `Materials.js` files under `--- medieval ---` and which
  `shadeStoneCastle` builds as a 5 × 3 grid of dressed ashlar blocks with mortar joints,
  chamfered arrises, tool marks and **lichen**.

  **Every prop on every planet drew with it** — 15,700 boulders, spires, slabs, shards,
  columns and vents across ten worlds, an ice moon, a salt pan and a lava field included, all
  wearing masonry courses and green lichen.

It was invisible for exactly the reason `art-space`'s squared albedo was: at the distance the
preset framings photograph a prop it is a few pixels, and the spherical UVs stretched the
coursing into vagueness. **Giving the geometry honest UVs is what made it legible.**

`rock.neutral` is the fix and it is not a new idea — `Materials.js` files it under
`--- planet ground ---`, **nine of the ten planets already use it for their terrain**, and its
own comment calls it "an honest description of regolith, volcanic ash, iron hardpan, salt
crust, a drained sea bed and crystalline gravel alike". Identical build options to
`dirt.ground`; the two materials differ in exactly one property, `envMapIntensity` 0.7 → 0.6.
Repeat left at 1.4 deliberately, so the measurement has one variable in it.

## 5. What was authored, and the number that was refused

### 5.1 Authored — `public/assets/planets/ejecta-block.glb`

Through the route proven six times, copied from `make-belt-glb.mjs` and `BeltAssets.js`:
committed generator reproducing the file byte for byte, `manifest.json` with
licence/source/parts/triangles/bytes, lazy loader with an abort signal and a synchronous
null-returning cache read, byte-diff test, glTF material discarded unread, documented graceful
degradation, and a row in `docs/assets/LICENCES.md`.

**The shape:** 20 triangles, unit radius, flat-shaded with per-face vertices. Three octaves of
deterministic value noise on the radius, biased inward so the body has *bays* rather than
bulges; two seeded clipping planes on non-opposed axes; per-face UV islands at a seeded offset
and a seeded rotation.

**The cost rule, which is the whole design.** The block **replaces the primitive inside the
field's existing `InstancedMesh`**. It does not earn a bucket:

> no renderable, no instanced mesh, no draw call, no material, no shader program, no light,
> no collider — **and no triangles**.

That is a stronger position than `make-belt-glb.mjs` could take. The belt had to buy a fourth
bucket because it wanted hero detail for 44 rocks out of 260 and an `InstancedMesh` carries one
geometry. Here every boulder in the game is the same case, so there is nothing to partition.

**Which boulders get it** is therefore not a threshold anybody invented: it is `kind:
'boulders'`, the descriptor's own word for what the thing is, and `planet-assets.test.mjs`
asserts the drawn set is the field's whole instance set off the real descriptors.

### 5.2 The placement rule that was measured and thrown away

`Belt.HERO_RADIUS` worked because the belt's population genuinely split. The equivalent rule
here was **measured before it was believed**: `CONFIG.player.stepHeight` is 0.45 m,
`StationAuditMath.js:41` states that a body steps over anything shorter, and a boulder's
collider stands `0.58 * sy` proud of the ground — so "a boulder you cannot walk over" selects

| planet/field | hero % | | planet/field | hero % |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cinder/ejecta | 91.8 | | verdigris/deadfall | 87.0 |
| tessera/rimblocks | 95.4 | | lathe/ejecta | 87.0 |
| shoal/storm_blocks | 85.0 | | carnelian/lag | 67.3 |

**7,369 of 8,880 — 83%.** That is not a partition, and at even 180 triangles a hero it would
have been +1.18 M triangles across the ten planets. A real published rule that turns out to
select almost everything is still the wrong rule. Recorded as a costed dead end.

### 5.3 The tessellation experiment, and its stopping rule

Both arms were built and photographed **on the same two boulders from the same cameras**.
`tall-three-quarter` is seven metres from the tallest ejecta block on Cinder, filling a
1600 × 900 frame — the framing most favourable to the extra triangles that exists in this
world.

| | 20 triangles | 58 triangles |
|---|---|---|
| whole-frame mean luma | 30.07 | 29.33 |
| whole-frame Sobel edge energy | **1.48** | **1.45** |
| cost on Cinder | 0 | **+26,400 (+13.8%)** |

A 0.03 change in edge energy, in the shot built to flatter it, for 13.8% of the world's
triangles in every frame. `art-space`'s protocol is "adopt only if the pixels move". They do
not. **Refused**, with the pair kept as `work-20-triangles-tall.jpg` /
`work-rejected-58-triangles-tall.jpg`.

Worth recording: 8 splits is **58** triangles, not the 44 the arithmetic suggests. Splitting a
face into four puts a midpoint on each of its three edges and the neighbour across each of
those edges has to close against it, so eight red faces drag green refinement into their
neighbours.

### 5.4 A claim of my own that the gate caught

The generator, the manifest and the licence row all said the block carried "two seeded
fracture planes shearing flats through it". **The bytes do not support it.** A plane at offset
0.80 catches a 37° cap and an icosahedron's twelve vertices are **63.4° apart**, so at most one
is ever clipped and no two facets come out coplanar — measured off the committed file, most
parallel pair 0.877 against a regular icosahedron's 0.7947, **zero coplanar pairs**.

A broad shear facet is simply not expressible at this tessellation; `make-belt-glb.mjs` gets
one because it has 500 triangles to cut across. All three now say what the planes actually buy,
which is asymmetry, and a **negative assertion** pins the limitation so that raising the split
count fails the test and points at the comment that has to change with it.

### 5.5 The first build had a hole in it, and nothing in the engine would have said so

The red-green refinement in §5.3 is not tidiness. The first version split eight faces into four
and left their neighbours spanning the old edge — a T-junction. Because the new midpoints are
re-projected onto the noise surface rather than left on the chord, that is **a triangular crack
straight through into the inside of the rock**, on eight faces of every one of 8,880 boulders.
The generator's closed-manifold gate caught it before a byte shipped.

### 5.6 Left procedural, deliberately

The other six prop families, the terrain, the liquid, the minerals, the landing pads, the sky
and the atmosphere. In particular **the terrain is 68.4% of the world and is not touched**:
re-authoring a 131,072-triangle generated surface is a phase of its own, not a line item in
this one.

### 6.2 The +48 draw calls that was not real, and the trap it sets

An earlier after-run reported **+48 draw calls at `lava-shore`**, and it reproduced on a second

| arm | lava-shore draws |
|---|---|
| base, as 4th framing, three separate runs | 131, 131, 131 |
| asset + `stone.castle`, lava-shore **alone** | 131 |
| asset + `rock.neutral`, lava-shore **alone** | 131 |

## 7. Gates

`npm test` — **3,169 pass, 0 fail**, of which 21 are the new file.
`node scripts/contract-check.mjs` — 129/129, all contracts satisfied.
`npm run build` — clean.

New: `scripts/tests/planet-assets.test.mjs` (21). **No existing test was edited.**

Worth naming, because the roadmap's line is that a gate measuring something the game does not
do is worse than no gate:

- **The degenerate-normal gate bites harder here than on the belt.** `belt-assets.test.mjs`
  records that the belt's material carries `flatShading: true`, so three ignores the stored
  normals and recomputes per fragment. The planet prop material is the **other** case:
  `flatShading` is false and the geometry carries its own per-face normals, so a zero-length
  one reaches `normalize()` in the **vertex** shader and NaNs every fragment the triangle
  covers, on 8,880 instances across ten worlds.
- **Winding is checked by closed-manifold + positive signed volume, not by a star-shaped facing
  test** — `art-maze`'s candle flame, and this body has clipping planes whose faces legitimately
  lean off radial.
- **A real Cinder ejecta field is built BOTH ways** and the test asserts every instance matrix
  and every collider box is **identical** and only the geometry differs. The headless suite
  takes the degraded arm, which is the arm that has to keep working when the file is missing.
- **A columns field is built with the block installed** and asserted unchanged, so a loader
  returning the part under the wrong key cannot silently turn every column, spire and slab in
  the game into a rock.
- **A negative assertion** pins the coplanarity limitation of §5.4.

## 10. Handed to the orchestrator

### 10.1 The atmosphere-shell experiment: NOT DONE, and why

`art-space` §8 left a costed experiment — the 8 atmosphere shells are 48,128 triangles, 33% of
open space, on a soft rim function that would probably be pixel-identical at 32×24 — and asked
this branch to take it or refuse it on evidence.

**Refused, and the evidence is the boundary rather than the pixels.** `BodyShaders.js` lives in
`src/worlds/space/**`, which this branch's file boundary names explicitly as another world's.
Running the experiment means editing that file, re-shooting **fifteen space framings** to
measure the sky side and then re-shooting a planet surface to measure the ground side, and
`belt-assets.test.mjs` carries a tripwire asserting open space owns exactly one authored asset.
`art-space` refused it because it was an unphotographed hypothesis in shaders a sibling might
want; this branch did not want them, and taking a cross-world shader change on the last of nine
world branches would be exactly the risk that staging Phase 9 one world at a time exists to
prevent. **The experiment is still open and still worth doing, by whoever owns both sides.**

### 10.2 Defects found that nothing tests

**a. 74.2% of every boulder's triangles are drawn below the terrain.** Measured on Cinder
against the world's own terrain bed (`_bed`, not `physics.groundHeight` — that raycast hits
each boulder's *own collider box* first and reports a false 98.7%): **16,330 of the ejecta
field's 22,000 triangles, 8.5% of the whole world**, and ~131,000 across the ten planets.

The obvious recovery — tessellate the visible side and cap the buried one — **is not
available**, and checking that before building saved the work: `buildPropField`'s boulder
branch applies a full 3-D tumble (`rotX = a·π`, `rotZ = c·π`), so there is no fixed buried
side. Recovering it needs the tumble removed, which changes how the field reads and is a
placement change, not an art change. Left alone and written down.

**b. Half of every `shards` instance is a base cap that is always underground.**
`ConeGeometry(1, 1, 4, 1)` is 4 lateral triangles and 4 base triangles; the base sits at the
instance origin and every shard is sunk `sy * 0.18` below it. Measured 50.5% buried on Cinder's
340-instance `glass` field. An open-ended cone would halve the family's triangles for a
pixel-identical result — 5,080 triangles across the four shard fields. **Not done**: it is a
second family, it wants its own before/after, and this pass had one asset in it.

**c. The lava lake's drawn edge, unresolved.** `framing-toe-lake-proposed.jpg` shows the toe
lake's surface meeting its banks on hard straight diagonals with what reads as a gap under the
lip. It may be correct — the descriptor builds that bed as a `pad`, so the shoreline *is* a
contour of a flat plane — and `art-dock`'s rule is not to change anything on one screenshot
read. **Recorded as observed and not diagnosed**, with the framing that shows it.

**d. `harness-framings.test.mjs` has no near floor.** §8.1. A framing pointed at the ground
three metres away passes a `subject: 55` declaration. This is the third occurrence of the shape
(`village-street` in `art-medieval`, `rimhold` here) and the fix is one clause in that test.

### 10.3 For the sibling branches

- **`HARNESS.look(..., { movePlayer: false })` is SAFE in planet worlds**, unlike in `space`.
  `PlanetWorld._buildSky` passes `params.camera`, so the dome re-centres on the **camera**
  every frame; `SpaceWorld`'s `Backdrop` is placed against the **player**, which is what gave
  `art-space` a black ellipse eating the star field. All three `--subject` headings produce
  valid frames here.
- **The whole-frame draw-call number is contaminated too**, not just `programs`. §6.2.
- **Check what a probe's own ray hits before believing it.** `physics.groundHeight` under a
  prop returns that prop's collider, not the ground. It turned a real 74% into a false 98.7%.
- **Making a thing look worse can be the diagnosis.** The masonry courses were invisible until
  honest UVs stopped stretching them. §4.
